What is Ayurveda?

Ayurveda is a traditional system of medicine from India. It treats health as a balance between body, mind and daily habits, and describes each person's natural constitution, called prakriti, through three doshas: vata (movement), pitta (digestion and metabolism) and kapha (structure and moisture).

An Ayurveda doctor combines your constitution with your current symptoms, diet, sleep, stress and the season to suggest food choices, daily and seasonal routines, and, where appropriate, herbal medicines. The aim is to correct the pattern behind a problem as well as the symptom itself.

Digestion and gut health

Acidity, bloating, constipation, irregular appetite and food habits.

Skin and hair

Dryness, breakouts, hair fall and routines that support healthy skin and scalp.

Everyday stiffness and lifestyle advice, alongside any treatment you already receive.

Stress, sleep and energy

Tiredness, restless sleep, and daily routines that support balance.

Weight and metabolic wellness

Diet and lifestyle guidance for healthy weight, used with your regular medical care.

Women's wellness

Menstrual health, comfort through life stages, and general wellbeing advice.

Seasonal and daily routine

Food and habits suited to your constitution and the time of year.

Long-standing conditions

Supportive lifestyle care next to your regular treatment, never a replacement for it.

When to go to a hospital instead

Online consultation is not for emergencies. Go to the nearest hospital or call your local emergency number for chest pain, difficulty breathing, severe bleeding, sudden weakness or numbness, loss of consciousness, a high fever in an infant, serious pregnancy concerns, or thoughts of harming yourself.

Using Ayurvedic medicines safely

  • Tell your doctor about all medicines and supplements you take. Some herbs can interact with medicines for diabetes, blood pressure or blood thinning.
  • Do not stop or change a medicine prescribed by another doctor without asking that doctor.
  • Some herbs are not suitable during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or for young children and older adults. Mention these at the start.
  • Some traditional preparations should be used only under a qualified practitioner's guidance and for the duration advised.
  • Avoid starting herbal products on your own, and buy medicines from licensed sources.

Ayurveda consultation: common questions

Is online Ayurveda consultation effective?
Online consultation works well for lifestyle, diet, routine and long-term wellness guidance, and for follow-ups. A doctor can ask detailed questions about your symptoms, digestion, sleep and habits, but cannot do a physical examination, so some problems need an in-person visit or tests. Research on Ayurvedic treatments varies in quality, so for serious or long-standing conditions it is best used alongside regular medical care, not instead of it.

Can I take Ayurvedic medicines with my allopathic medicines?
Some herbs can interact with prescription medicines, such as those for diabetes, blood pressure or blood thinning. Always tell your Ayurveda doctor about every medicine and supplement you take, and do not stop or change a prescribed medicine without speaking to the doctor who prescribed it.
What is prakriti?
In Ayurveda, prakriti is a person's natural constitution, described by the balance of three doshas: vata, pitta and kapha. Practitioners use it, together with your current symptoms, habits and season, to shape diet and lifestyle advice.
What should I keep ready before my consultation?
Keep a list of your symptoms and how long you have had them, your current medicines and supplements, any allergies, recent reports or prescriptions, and a note of your usual sleep, meals and daily routine. If you are pregnant or breastfeeding, tell the doctor at the start.
Can I order Ayurvedic medicines after the consultation?
Yes. After your consultation you can order medicines and herbal products in the Healthy Tantra app as advised by your doctor. Use them only as directed, and avoid starting herbal products on your own.
Is online Ayurveda consultation suitable for emergencies?
No. If you have chest pain, trouble breathing, severe bleeding, sudden weakness or numbness, loss of consciousness, a high fever in an infant, or thoughts of harming yourself, go to the nearest hospital or call your local emergency number immediately.
